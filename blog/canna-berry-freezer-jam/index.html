Canna-Berry Freezer Jam with Raspberries

A bright raspberry jam that doubles as an easy-to-portion edible: the infusion goes in after cooking, so the math per tablespoon stays simple.

Key takeaways

  • Cook the jam to its gel point first, then stir in the infusion after about 5 minutes off the heat.
  • Total mg in the infusion divided by the tablespoons of jam gives the dose per spoonful.
  • Glycerin and alcohol tinctures mix in easily; coconut oil firms up when chilled and can show as flecks.
  • Keep it in the fridge or freezer, never the pantry, and label every jar.

This cannabis jam recipe makes a small batch of raspberry freezer jam and stirs the infusion in only after cooking. That spares it the hard boil, and measuring the finished jam first keeps the dose per tablespoon simple.

Dosing: total mg divided by tablespoons

Divide the THC in your infusion by the tablespoons of finished jam. This batch makes about 2 1/2 cups, or 40 tablespoons, so 100 mg gives roughly 2.5 mg per level tablespoon; half of a tincture labeled 200 mg per bottle would do it. Stir it in for a full minute, then count one level tablespoon as a serving and give it at least 2 hours; our THC dosage guide explains the wait.

What you need

Raspberries, fresh or thawed frozen, sugar warmed in a low oven so it dissolves fast, and lemon juice. For the infusion, use a glycerin or alcohol tincture or melted canna-coconut oil.

The cannabis jam recipe, step by step

Bring the berries to a boil, mashing as they soften, then add the warm sugar and lemon juice and boil hard, stirring often. Start testing after about 5 minutes. The jam is ready at 220°F at sea level, minus 2°F per 1,000 feet of elevation. Or spoon a little onto a cold plate and set it in the freezer for a few minutes; if it gels, the jam is ready. Let it cool off the heat for 5 minutes, then stir in the infusion.

Choosing an infusion

THC dissolves in alcohol, glycerin and fats, so each one carries it into a jam that's mostly fruit, sugar and water.

InfusionTasteTexture
Glycerin tinctureSweet, mildMixes in easily
Alcohol tinctureSharper, a little boozyMixes in easily
Canna-coconut oilMildFirms up and can fleck when cold

Storage and labeling

This isn't a canning recipe, so keep the jam in the fridge or freezer. Ohio State University Extension gives uncooked freezer jams up to three weeks refrigerated or a year frozen, and an opened jar a few days. If you used infused oil, freeze every jar but the open one. Mark each lid "infused," with the mg per tablespoon and the date, and keep the jars apart from your regular jam, out of reach of curious kids and pets.

Recipe

Makes
About 2 1/2 cups (40 tablespoons)
Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
20 minutes

Ingredients

  • 4 cups raspberries, fresh or thawed frozen
  • 2 cups granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • Your measured infusion: an alcohol or glycerin tincture, or melted canna-coconut oil (see the note)

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 250°F and warm the sugar in a baking dish for 10 minutes. Put a small plate in the freezer.
  2. Bring the raspberries to a boil in a wide, heavy pot over medium-high heat, mashing as they soften.
  3. Stir in the warm sugar and lemon juice until dissolved, then boil hard, stirring often.
  4. After about 5 minutes, start testing: the jam is ready at 220°F at sea level (2°F lower per 1,000 feet), or when a spoonful on the cold plate gels after a few minutes in the freezer.
  5. Take the pot off the heat and let it cool for 5 minutes. Stir in the measured infusion for a full minute.
  6. Ladle into clean jars, leaving 1/2 inch of headspace, and let them cool. Label every jar, refrigerate one and freeze the rest.

Dosing note

Estimate only: 100 mg of THC stirred into about 40 tablespoons of jam is roughly 2.5 mg per level tablespoon. With a tincture, go by the label's THC total; homemade oil usually lands below its estimate, depending on the flower and on what decarbing and infusing took out. At that strength, one level tablespoon is a sensible first serving; wait at least 2 hours before having more. Mark every jar and keep them on the top shelf, behind anything kids reach for and away from pets.

Frequently asked questions

Why add the cannabis after cooking?

The jam boils hard to reach its gel point, and your infusion doesn't need that heat. Stirring it in after a few minutes off the burner spares it the boil, and measuring the finished jam first makes the dose per tablespoon easy to work out.

How much THC is in a spoonful?

Only your own numbers can say. Divide the total mg you stirred in by the tablespoons of finished jam: 100 mg through about 40 tablespoons gives roughly 2.5 mg per level tablespoon. Mix well before filling jars, then try a single level tablespoon and give it at least 2 hours.

Can infused jam be canned for the pantry?

No. This is a freezer jam, not a tested canning recipe, so it belongs in the fridge or freezer. A pantry shelf is also where an infused jar is most likely to be mistaken for regular jam. Freeze the extras and thaw one jar at a time.
